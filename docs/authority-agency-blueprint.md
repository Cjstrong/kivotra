## 0. First principles — assumptions challenged, services cut

**Assumption 1: "More press = more authority." Rejected.** Authority is trust transferred from a source the buyer already respects, compounded by consistency over time. One feature the buyer believes beats ten they discount. Consequence: we sell a portfolio with provenance, not placement volume.

**Assumption 2: The tabloid inventory builds authority. Rejected — cut from the core.** InTouch, Star, Closer, National Enquirer, National Examiner, First For Women, Globe are celebrity/gossip mastheads. For an Irish founder selling considered purchases, a National Enquirer byline is at best irrelevant and at worst corrosive — it reads as bought the moment a buyer checks. This inventory comes off the rate card. It survives only as unlisted, by-request inventory for US-facing consumer/entertainment brands, and it is never sold as "authority building" — it is sponsored lifestyle coverage and we call it that.

**Assumption 3: US logos alone convince Irish buyers. Half-true.** "Featured in USA Today / Entrepreneur" works at badge level (logo bar, LinkedIn banner, pitch deck). It fails on inspection if it's all there is. The trust ceiling for Irish customers is Irish media, Irish institutions, awards, speaking. So: **paid placements are the floor** — fast, guaranteed, controllable. **Earned media is the ceiling** — slow, never guaranteed. We guarantee the floor and pursue the ceiling, in writing, in every proposal.

**Assumption 4: An agency sells deliverables. Rejected.** We sell a measurable asset: the client's Authority Score and the portfolio behind it. Authority decays without maintenance and compounds with it — which is why the retainer is real economics, not a billing habit.

**Assumption 5: "AI-native" means writing faster with AI. Rejected.** It means two deeper things. (a) Margin structure: AI does all first drafts, research, monitoring, and scoring; humans do only judgment, relationships, and standards — that's 65–70% gross margin at boutique headcount. (b) New surface: buyers now ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity who to trust. What AI systems say about a founder is an authority surface no traditional agency services. We measure and build for it — without ever promising control over model outputs.

**Services cut or folded (from the original eight):**

- **Reputation building (standalone) — cut.** Reputation is the outcome of everything else. Sold alone it drifts into reputation-management/suppression work, which we refuse: swamp economics, ethical hazard.
- **Content strategy (standalone) — folded into retainers.** Strategy without an execution engine is shelfware.
- **Founder branding + executive positioning — merged** into one service, Founder Positioning. Same craft, different seniority of subject.
- **Website authority optimisation — kept but narrowed** to authority pages only: newsroom/press page, about page, founder pages, structured data. We are not an SEO agency and never sell rankings.

**The honesty standard (our moat).** The placement-reseller market is riddled with inflated claims ("get featured in Yahoo Finance!"). The agency that tells the truth about exactly what $1,500 buys becomes the trusted one — which is the only acceptable brand behaviour for a company selling trust. Concretely: the three-lane guarantee table (§5), the "Featured in / Distributed via" language rule, and the never-promise list (Appendix) appear in every proposal and contract.

## 1. Company positioning

**One-liner: "Kivotra makes you trusted before the meeting."**

Positioning statement: For founders and executives of high-trust Irish businesses, Kivotra is the authority agency that builds a verifiable portfolio of trust assets — editorial features, press infrastructure, founder positioning, and owned authority pages — measured quarterly by an Authority Score, produced AI-first to human editorial standards. Unlike PR agencies, we guarantee what is guaranteeable and say plainly what is not. Unlike marketing agencies, we are not paid to chase attention; we are paid to compound trust.

The three sentences we repeat everywhere:

1. We are not a marketing agency. Marketing rents attention.
2. We are not a PR agency. PR sells effort.
3. We build authority: the asset that makes buyers arrive already convinced.

## 2. Unique market category

**Category name: Authority Infrastructure.** Kivotra is Ireland's first authority agency — the company that installs it.

Category point of view (the keynote, the sales narrative, the About page): *Every buyer now runs two background checks before they talk to you — a human one on Google and an AI one through ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity. Most Irish founders fail both, not because they lack substance but because they lack infrastructure.* Authority infrastructure has four layers:

1. **Narrative layer** — positioning, story, claims backed by evidence.
2. **Proof layer** — third-party editorial, transparently sourced.
3. **Signal layer** — press distribution footprint and newsroom.
4. **Measurement layer** — the Authority Score, including AI-answer monitoring.

Category-king move: define the measurement standard (§15) and publish proprietary data (the Kivotra Irish Founder Trust Index, §20) so the market adopts our vocabulary.

## 3. Ideal client profile

**Primary ICP — "the invisible expert":**

- Founder, MD, or partner of an Irish B2B or professional-services firm: 5–50 staff, €500k–€10M revenue
- Sells considered purchases: €10k+ deals, or high-trust consumer (clinics, advisory, legal, financial services, energy/construction, tech)
- Genuinely excellent at the work; the digital footprint doesn't show it — page 1 for their name is LinkedIn and a directory listing
- Trigger events: fundraising, UK/US market entry, award season, keynote or book, senior hiring push, acquisition positioning
- Economic buyer is the founder themself; sales cycle 1–4 weeks

**Secondary ICP** (only after 10 primary clients): US-facing consumer/e-commerce brands — the one segment where the lifestyle inventory can genuinely serve, sold as sponsored lifestyle coverage.

**Disqualifiers — we refuse:**

- Wants guaranteed rankings, traffic, leads, or sales
- Wants negative coverage buried or reviews suppressed
- Makes claims they can't evidence (especially health or financial)
- Operates in sectors our outlets won't accept (many programmes refuse gambling, CBD, adult, some crypto) — checked before contract, not after
- Wants paid placements passed off as earned coverage

## 4. Customer journey

Seven stages; every stage ends with a scored artifact so progress is legible and the next purchase is obvious.

1. **Stranger** → encounters our own authority in action (the dogfooding flywheel, §9) or receives a Snapshot.
2. **Snapshot** (free, automated): "How Google and AI describe you today" — a 2-page, agent-generated gap report, human-reviewed before sending.
3. **Audit** (€1,250): full Authority Score baseline + 90-day roadmap. Credited against the Launchpad. Standalone value even if they never hire us again.
4. **Launchpad** (8 weeks, €9,500): the foundation installed.
5. **Retainer**: monthly compounding.
6. **Quarterly Score review**: renewal is built on the measured delta in assets and surfaces — never revenue attribution.
7. **Expansion**: second executive, new market, UK entry.

## 5. Service catalogue — three lanes and the guarantee table

**Lane A — Editorial Features (paid placements).** Premium inventory: USA Today, Entrepreneur.com, Complex.com (hard cost $1,500 each). What it is: sponsored/contributor programmes where publication is guaranteed subject to the outlet's editorial standards, disclosed according to each outlet's policy. Includes angle development, interview, writing, two revision rounds, submission, and an amplification kit. What it is not: earned journalism — and we never present it as such. Lifestyle inventory (InTouch, Star, etc.): unlisted, by request, consumer brands only.

**Lane B — Press Release Distribution.** Networks: Yahoo/Accesswire, Yahoo Finance syndication, GlobeNewswire, Newsfile, EIN Presswire, GetNews, premium DA80+ and high-DA subdomains, Financial News Network. Guaranteed: professional drafting plus distribution across the chosen network, with a syndication URL report. Not guaranteed: journalist pickup, indexing, traffic. The honest use case: the public record — funding, launches, hires, data — and a consistent footprint that human and AI diligence finds.

**Lane C — Authority Systems (owned assets and ongoing work).**

- Founder Positioning Sprint: narrative, claims-with-evidence, bio suite, media kit, speaker one-pager
- Authority pages: newsroom/press page, about, founder pages, structured data (narrow scope, no SEO promises)
- Founder content engine: ghostwritten columns and LinkedIn, with approval gates
- Earned-media pursuit (top tier only): journalist-request monitoring, pitching, award and speaking submissions — explicitly best-effort
- Authority Score monitoring, quarterly

**The guarantee table** (verbatim in every proposal):

| Lane | You are paying for | Guaranteed | Never guaranteed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Editorial | A written feature published on a named outlet via its sponsored/contributor programme | Publication (subject to outlet standards), disclosure per outlet policy, timeline | That it reads as earned; rankings; traffic |
| Distribution | Drafting + syndication across a named network | Distribution + URL report | Journalist coverage; indexing; traffic |
| Systems | Positioning, assets, content, monitoring | The assets themselves, on time, to standard | Sales; followers; virality |

**Language standard:** "Featured in" is reserved for editorial features. "Distributed via" is the only phrasing for wire syndication. We never let a client claim otherwise with our name attached.

## 7. Pricing model

- **Fixed prices, published.** No hourly billing anywhere — AI-native margins die the day you sell hours.
- **Hard costs bundled, disclosed on request.** Premium editorial costs us ~€1,400 at current FX (with 10% buffer); we don't hide it, because transparency is the brand. We charge for the strategy, craft, and guarantee around the placement, not for access alone.
- **Payment terms:** projects 50% on signature, 50% at midpoint. Retainers monthly in advance, quarterly minimum. Placements are not submitted until paid — we pay vendors on order.
- **No discounts.** Price integrity is authority behaviour. Annual prepay earns a bonus editorial feature, not a percentage off.
- **Margin targets:** ≥70% on Lane C work, ≥55% blended on anything containing placements, ≥65% blended company-wide.

## 8. Delivery SOPs

The spine: every client gets one **Authority Ledger** (Notion): narrative bank, evidence locker, asset log, coverage log, score history. Six standard SOPs run against it:

1. **Intake & narrative extraction** — recorded 75-minute founder interview → agent-extracted narrative bank (20+ stories, every claim linked to evidence) → human verification. SLA: 5 working days.
2. **Editorial production** (§14). SLA: 3–4 weeks from angle approval.
3. **Release production** (§13). SLA: 5 working days from facts doc.
4. **Asset production** — bios, kits, authority pages. SLA: 10 working days.
5. **Monthly reporting** — agent-drafted from the ledger, 15-minute human pass.
6. **Quarterly Score review** — agent-computed, human-presented.

Three gates on everything that leaves the building: evidence check, named human sign-off, written client approval. Two revision rounds standard.

## 9. Sales system

**Flywheel first: Kivotra is client zero.** We run our own Launchpad publicly — our Score, our coverage log, a monthly build-log. The agency's own authority is the demo, and it stress-tests every SOP before a client pays for it.

Three channels:

1. **Founder-led outbound:** 20 Snapshots/week to named ICP founders — agent-researched and drafted, human-reviewed and sent. The Snapshot *is* the cold email: personal, useful, zero pitch.
2. **Partnerships:** accountants, VC/PE platform teams, Enterprise Ireland and LEO networks, chambers, founder communities. 10% referral fee on year one of referred contracts.
3. **Authority marketing:** the Trust Index (§20), talks, the build-log.

**The sales call** (45 minutes, one call to Audit): gap walkthrough → what good looks like in their category → the Score → one recommendation. We sell the Audit, not the retainer; the Audit sells the retainer.

Rules: no free strategy, no spec work, no proposal over 3 pages, no discounts.

## 10. CRM pipeline

Stages with exit criteria: **Identified** (ICP fit, evidence noted) → **Snapshot sent** → **Conversation** (call booked) → **Audit sold** → **Audit delivered** (fork: Proposal / Nurture) → **Proposal** (3-pager, 14-day validity) → **Won** → **Onboarding** → **Active** → **Renewal/Expansion**. Losses coded: price / timing / wanted-guarantees / disqualified. "Wanted-guarantees" losses are wins — log them proudly.

Run it in Notion from day one; agents handle enrichment, stall alerts (no reply in 7 days), and follow-up drafts.

The Year-1 machine, modelled: 20 snapshots/week → 10% to conversation ≈ 8 calls/mo → 40% to Audit ≈ 3 audits/mo → 50% to Launchpad/retainer ≈ **1.5 new clients/mo**. That's the whole engine.

## 11. Proposal template (3 pages, agent-generated from the Audit, human-tuned)

- **Page 1 — Where you stand:** their Score wheel, top 3 gaps, what diligence currently finds (screenshots, including one AI answer).
- **Page 2 — The plan:** one recommended package plus one smaller alternative; the guarantee table; 90-day timeline; what we need from them.
- **Page 3 — Investment & terms:** price, payment schedule, quarterly commitment, mutual refusal rights, and the never-promise list as a counter-signed page.

Having clients counter-sign the never-promise list is the single strongest trust move in the entire sales process.

## 12. Client onboarding — 14 days to first win

- **Day 0:** contract + invoice + access checklist (brand assets, headshots, permission to name clients/metrics, comms channel).
- **Days 1–3:** founder interview booked and held; evidence locker opened.
- **Day 5:** narrative bank delivered — the "you know me better than my last agency" moment.
- **Day 7:** kickoff — angles approved, calendar agreed, one named approver with a 48-hour SLA.
- **Day 10:** first asset live (bio suite + press page copy, or first release).
- **Day 14:** first-win review; editorial interview scheduled.

Rule: if the client misses their approval SLA twice, a founder calls. Approval lag is the #1 delivery killer and the #1 churn predictor.

## 13. Press workflow (distribution lane)

1. Trigger calendar built at onboarding: launches, hires, funding, partnerships, data — plus quarterly **manufactured news**: original mini-research from client data or commissioned surveys, newsworthy on merit.
2. Facts doc from client (agent-templated).
3. Agent drafts in house style; human editor pass; evidence gate.
4. One client approval round.
5. Network tier chosen by purpose (financial news → Newsfile/GlobeNewswire; general → Accesswire/EIN; etc.).
6. Distribute; collect syndication URLs; log to ledger.
7. Report plainly: "distributed via X, syndicated to N pages" — never "featured in Yahoo Finance."

SLA: 5 working days, facts doc to live.

## 14. Editorial workflow (feature lane)

1. Three angle memos from the narrative bank (agent); human picks two to present.
2. Client selects; interview questions issued; 20-minute supplementary call if needed.
3. Draft: AI-assisted from transcript + narrative bank; senior human edit for voice and judgment; evidence gate on every claim.
4. Client approval, two rounds.
5. Submission per outlet programme rules. The outlet's editorial review may request changes or decline a topic — clients are told this at kickoff and an alternative angle is pre-agreed.
6. Publication verified; URL and disclosure status logged.
7. Amplification kit within 48 hours: 3 LinkedIn posts, pull-quote graphics, "Featured in" badge usage rules, newsroom update.

SLA: 3–4 weeks from angle approval, stated per outlet.

## 15. Authority Score framework (0–100, quarterly)

Five pillars, 20 points each:

1. **Narrative & Owned** — positioning clarity, bio consistency across surfaces, authority pages, structured data, evidence-backed claims ratio.
2. **Editorial Proof** — features weighted by outlet credibility and recency. Earned outweighs paid roughly 3:1, and we say so openly — that's what makes the score believable.
3. **Distribution Footprint** — release cadence, syndication density, newsroom completeness, milestone coverage.
4. **Search Surface** — share of page-1 results for founder and brand that are controlled or positive; presence of authoritative third-party results.
5. **AI Answer Surface** — do ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity know them; accuracy; sentiment; quality of cited sources. Measured monthly by an agent battery of standardised prompts. We report it and build the inputs; we never claim control of model outputs.

Printed on every score: *this measures authority assets and visibility surfaces, not revenue.* The Score is the product spine: the Audit sells with it, the retainer renews on its delta, the Trust Index aggregates it, and Year 3 productises it.

## 16. Profit model

Revenue mix target Y1: retainers 60% / launchpads 30% / audits + add-ons 10%. Loaded human hour €60; AI cost <€50/client/mo (rounding error).

| Product | Price | Main costs | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audit | €1,250 | ~6 hrs + agents | ~70% (it's a paid lead, not a profit centre) |
| Launchpad | €9,500 | ~€3,000 hard (2 editorials + wire) + ~35 hrs | ~55% |
| Presence | €2,750/mo | ~10 hrs + wire | ~72% |
| Authority | €4,750/mo | ~14 hrs + €467/mo editorial amortised | ~65% |
| Market Leader | €8,500/mo | ~24 hrs + €1,400/mo editorial | ~66% |

Break-even: fixed base (two modest founder salaries + tools ≤ €9k/mo) ≈ **3 Authority-tier clients**. Year-1 exit target: 12–15 retainers, €45k MRR, blended GM ≥65%.

Profit rules: no hourly billing; no custom scope (custom is margin leak — clients pick tiers); 10% of gross profit reinvested annually into Kivotra's own authority and the Trust Index — marketing that appreciates.

## 17. Hiring roadmap

- **Phase 1 (now → €25k MRR): founders only.** One owns revenue (snapshots, calls, partnerships); one owns delivery (editorial standards, SOPs, agents). Both write.
- **Hire 1 (~€25k MRR): senior editor**, part-time to full. An ex-journalist. The quality gate is the one thing AI cannot own; buy the best judgment we can afford.
- **Hire 2 (~15 retainers): placements/ops coordinator** — vendor wrangling, scheduling, ledger hygiene, report QA.
- **Hire 3 (Year 2): partnerships & accounts lead.**

**Never hire:** junior copywriters (agents draft), social media managers, SEO staff, account managers before 20 clients — founders owning relationships *is* the product.

Rule: every hire must either raise the editorial standard or free founder hours for selling and relationships. Nothing else gets headcount.

## 18. AI automations (build order)

Principle: **AI produces, humans approve.** Nothing external ships without a named human sign-off. Each automation must remove ≥5 founder-hours/week or gate a risk.

1. **Snapshot agent** — prospect name/company → SERP snapshot + standardised AI-answer battery → 2-page gap report draft. (The sales engine.)
2. **Narrative extractor** — interview transcript → structured story/claim/evidence bank. (The delivery spine.)
3. **Draft agents** — editorial, release, and LinkedIn formats, tuned to house style.
4. **Evidence gate** — cross-checks every claim in any draft against the locker; flags anything unsupported. (The never-overpromise policy, enforced in software.)
5. **Score computer** — monthly surface crawls, quarterly Score assembly.
6. **Monitoring** — new mentions, coverage, AI-answer drift; alerts.
7. **CRM agents** — enrichment, stall alerts, follow-up drafts.
8. **Reporting agent** — monthly client report from the ledger.

Later (Y2–3): self-serve Score product; partner-facing snapshot portal.

## 19. KPI dashboard

- **Weekly (company):** MRR, retainer count, pipeline coverage (3× target), snapshots sent, calls held, audits sold, win/loss reasons.
- **Monthly (delivery):** on-time %, approval cycles per asset (target ≤2), placements delivered vs sold, editor hours per asset (the AI-leverage trend), client pulse.
- **Quarterly (client value):** average Authority Score delta, % of clients with score up, coverage log growth, search-surface control %, AI-answer accuracy, logo retention, net revenue retention.

One rule: client dashboards show assets and surfaces — things we control. Traffic and leads never appear on a client dashboard. Conversion metrics live on the internal dashboard only.

## Appendix — the never-promise list (in every contract)

We never promise: search rankings, indexing, traffic, leads, sales, virality, follower growth, earned coverage, specific AI model outputs, Wikipedia pages, Google knowledge panels, link permanence, or that paid placements will be perceived as earned.

We do promise: named deliverables, stated timelines, disclosure compliance, and evidence-backed claims.

Compliance notes (Ireland/EU): paid-for editorial must be identifiable as such (EU Unfair Commercial Practices rules; ASAI Code). We follow each outlet's disclosure convention and never strip labels. Client claims must be substantiated, especially health and financial claims.
